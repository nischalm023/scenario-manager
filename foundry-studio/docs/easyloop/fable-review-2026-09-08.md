## Findings

**1. Extraction ports are reserved from departure, have no approach queue, and are the binding resource. Fix before any capacity claim or the 20-hour experiment.** This is a modelling and dispatch structure problem, not a defect against a ruling, and it is verified by measurement. `Navigate::available_destination` at `sim/dessim/src/flows/transport/navigate.rs:135-192` skips any port carrying an open-ended hold by another robot, without looking at when that hold starts. The pathfinder commits a departing HTM's destination hold open-ended at its arrival time, so from the moment one HTM commits a path to a port, no other HTM may choose it until the holder's next path commits after the 9 s dwell. `navigate.rs:239-270` then waits at the HTM's current cell, holding it, until an oracle change. That cell is the order berth for SC and 3P orders and the buffer relay access cell for DS orders.

Measured in `headed-500.xlsx`, sheets `HTM Journey Legs` and `Extraction Operations`, taking each extraction's arriving leg start as commit and the robot's next leg start as release:

| Port | Extractions | Logical occupancy of run | Travel leg mean | At-port hold |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 17 | 161 | 92% | 21.5 s | 9.0 s |
| 18 | 174 | 92% | 19.4 s | 9.0 s |
| 19 | 165 | 91% | 20.5 s | 9.0 s |

All three ports were simultaneously reserved for 86% of that run. The physical port is busy only for the 9 s dwell; the resource report's 36% duty per port measures that dwell and hides the reservation. The consequences are the tails the throughput document already reports without a cause: direct orders in the same workbook hold their berth after the final pick for a mean of 171 s, median 26 s, p90 646 s, while DS orders leave in under one second. In `source-4000-final/source-long-orders.json` the last-pick-to-extraction-start interval for direct orders has mean 277 s and p95 797 s, and the buffer-HTM-pickup-to-extraction interval has mean 264 s and p95 810 s. The buffer relay access cells in zones 21 to 23 show 62 to 75% utilization in the final 4000 log because HTMs stand on them waiting for a port. The document's statement that significant outbound blocking remains, at `docs/easyloop/throughput.md:386-389`, is correct but does not name this mechanism, and the operational-analysis table at `throughput.md:99-124` has no row for extraction service. Recommendation: first add a port logical-occupancy measurement to the reporting path, then decide between declared approach cells at extraction stations, which mirrors the EasyLoop queue and is physically honest, and a finite port hold committed by `ExtractOrder` with an egress allowance. The site question is whether docking stations physically have an approach queue; it is not in `questions.html`.

**2. Every pick's inventory retrieval is anchored to the order HTM's planned berth arrival and each pick is fully serialized. Fix at will, but before any capacity claim.** `sim/dessim/src/flows/fulfillment/orders.rs:537-543` passes `order.action_ready_at` as the not-before time of the inventory retrieve, and `after` is a floor on the VTM cycle start in `RelayService::select_relay_for_retrieve`. The awaits at `orders.rs:538-544`, `603-615`, `634-643` and `694-696` run one pick at a time: retrieve, move to dropoff, admit, unload, present, approach, transfer, return to berth, then the next retrieve. So the VTM cycle, relay pickup, drive to the dropoff, unload and the 22.5 s inbound leg all happen while the order HTM sits on its berth. In `headed-500.xlsx` the order HTMs carrying their container waited at the pick station 495 times, mean 130.7 s, median 82.4 s, total 64,714 s in a 5,340 s run; the retained trip timelines show every order arriving at its berth and then standing 60 to 130 s before its presentation approach. With 24 berths the 4000-order run's berth cycle is about 251 s per single-pick order against roughly 25 s of approach, dwell and return. Recommendation: release the first inventory retrieval at claim time and prefetch the next line's tote during the current pick; `PresentTote` already gates admission on physical inventory readiness and `TransferItems` on order readiness, so the safety argument survives. The document lists only the presentation-approach overlap at `throughput.md:414-419`.
