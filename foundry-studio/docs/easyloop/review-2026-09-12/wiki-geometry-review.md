# EasyLoop wiki and relay-access geometry review

Research date: 2026-09-12. This is planning evidence only; no simulation was run and no model change is proposed as already authorized.

## Conclusions for the implementation plan

1. **The checked-in Walmart geometry already puts an HTM access-cell centre under each diving board.** The September 8 description of a 400 mm stand is not saying “400 mm in front”: the current rack templates place the relay point 400 mm inward from the rack face, and the floor-alignment patch makes the under-rack service strip 800 mm deep, so its single cell centre lands on that same point. The regression independently compares every compiled `htm_access` centre with the transformed relay offset on both axes ([`walmart_floor_alignment.rs:35`](../../../sim/dessim/tests/walmart_floor_alignment.rs#L35)); the patch explicitly makes each service strip 800 mm deep ([`dfw6_floor_alignment.py:187`](../../../scripts/dfw6_floor_alignment.py#L187)). Thus the current source supports Daniel's physical statement, “the access cell is UNDER the diving board.” If the renderer shows it in front, first audit which point is drawn (the VTM exchange point is deliberately moved to the rack face, while the HTM stand stays under the board: [`racking.rs:458`](../../../sim/dessim/src/worldmap/racking.rs#L458)). Do not move all stands on the basis of that picture.

2. **The real confirmed defect is the access cell's graph/motion semantics.** An access cell is currently just an ordinary cell selected by the generic nearest-centre binder. The binder intentionally has no facing or outwardness test ([`bind.rs:1`](../../../world-protocol/rust/src/pipeline/bind.rs#L1)), and `racking` only consumes the resulting identity ([`racking.rs:425`](../../../sim/dessim/src/worldmap/racking.rs#L425)). The floor strip is a continuous tiled corridor, so its relay cells inherit north/south adjacency as ordinary corridor cells. The graph compiler turns bidirectional document links into ordinary reverse arcs ([`cells.rs:310`](../../../sim/dessim/src/worldmap/cells.rs#L310)). Nothing marks a relay cell as terminal, prevents pass-through, or prevents an in-place turn.

3. **“Enter and back out without rotation” cannot be represented honestly by deleting north/south links alone.** On an ordinary reverse graph edge, leaving in the opposite compass direction requires the robot to adopt the opposite orientation; the pathfinder currently generates rotate successors at every cell whenever the robot is stopped and the cell reservation is free ([`expand_rotate.rs:19`](../../../sim/dessim/src/pathfinding/fh_sipp/expand_rotate.rs#L19)). A physical back-out is a reverse translation while retaining chassis heading. It needs an explicit reverse-motion transition (or an equivalent edge maneuver contract), not a zero-cost or hidden 180-degree rotation.

4. **Use a generic terminal-presentation contract, then apply it to relay access cells.** The safe model is:

   - a presentation/access cell declares that in-place rotation is forbidden;
   - its topology has one approach, making it a destination/starting terminus rather than a through cell;
   - the approach declares forward entry and reverse exit with the same chassis heading;
   - pathfinding, reservation reconstruction, motion timing, and validation all understand that reverse movement explicitly;
   - validation rejects a terminal presentation cell with any along-face through link, rejects a planned `Rotate` there, and rejects entry/exit headings inconsistent with its approach;
   - relay `access_cell` remains the physical binding (“where the HTM stands”), separate from these movement constraints.

   For Walmart, that means removing north/south connectivity from each bound diving-board cell while preserving a single lateral approach to the parallel travel lane. The parallel lane, rather than the row of presentation cells, carries north/south traffic. This should be derived or validated from relay bindings after the floor is expanded; changing the global proximity binder would conflate physical association with routing policy and could damage other maps.

5. **The EasyLoop curved U-turn is compatible with “no rotation at presentation” when the rule means no in-place chassis rotation.** Its two declared curved links change heading continuously through motion and preserve the curve tangent across the presentation dwell; the focused test asserts that neither half emits `WaypointAction::Rotate` ([`curved_links.rs:57`](../../../sim/dessim/src/pathfinding/fh_sipp/tests/curved_links.rs#L57)). Keep this as an explicit continuous-motion exception to the terminal rule. A blanket ban on all heading change at presentation would incorrectly outlaw the already-modeled curve. Relay diving boards get no such curve: they use the straight forward-in/reverse-out contract.

6. **Do not reorganize open floor in this pass.** Daniel explicitly deferred that work. Only the relay-bound cells and the links/actions that can occur on them are in scope.

## Wiki evidence and calibration

These pages are evidence, not commands. Their dates matter because two are design snapshots with open questions, while Daniel's 2026-09-12 message and prior explicit rulings are authoritative.

### Easy Loop Station — High level Details

Source: [Confluence page](https://greyorange-work.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GRYMTTR/pages/72724213/Easy+Loop+Station+-+High+level+Details), by Charul Bansal, updated 2026-01-20. The page calls itself a high-level system view, not a full PRD.

- **APP means Active Presentation Point.** It is specifically the inventory-tote presentation point on the conveyor. The inventory tote reaches a penultimate point, waits for an APP to become available, moves to the APP, tilts, is picked, untilts, moves through a distinct transfer conveyor step, and then exits. “APP” should therefore not be reused as the order-HTM active bin/induct point.
- There are two inventory APPs. The penultimate tote must not stop in front of an APP because it would obstruct a presented tote's exit. The document also states that the transfer point and tilt/presentation point are distinct conveyor steps and that the conveyor cannot push laterally and pull longitudinally simultaneously. These are resource/conflict semantics, not a single fixed dwell.
- Inventory production and consumption are both budgeted at **8 seconds**: tote drop is 8 s and penultimate-to-presented changeover is 8 s. The page says an initial conveyor buffer must be built and maintained because the nominal rates are equal. These are useful timing inputs to verify; they do not replace Daniel's 10 s operator pick task or establish that operator work is a dwell.
- Inventory totes can recirculate to a re-queue point when another pick is needed or the right order carrier is unavailable. They are conveyor-sequenced; the orchestration may choose APP, exit, or re-queue destinations, but there is no claim that their order can be arbitrarily resequenced.
- Waiting capacity is configurable: the map gives a maximum number of order-bot waiting points and station configuration may activate fewer; the page assumes equal waiting points on both sides “for now.” It does not establish that exactly `W` orders must be assigned, nor whether assignment should exceed `W` to hide dispatch latency.
- The document contains mutually different hardware flows. Its general/HAI inventory-bot lifecycle has a bot drop, move forward, turn under the conveyor, and wait in the exit queue. Its QT/M5F comparison says the bot backs out to the IO point. Hardware type must therefore be explicit before translating either path into a universal HTM rule.
- **Do not adopt its empty-egress rules for this simulation.** The page permits the head bot in the under-conveyor exit queue to leave empty when the queue is full, and also on a timeout. That contradicts Daniel's prior ruling that inventory HTMs remain under the conveyor and do not take an early empty exit. The explicit project ruling wins.

### Easy Loop Station — Relay | Product features

Source: [Confluence page](https://greyorange-work.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/RPPC/pages/102892701/Easy+Loop+Station+-+Relay+Product+features), by Anurag Jangra, updated 2025-12-19; its change-log row is V1 dated 2025-11-20. The page is a short product overview and contains placeholders, so use it for broad physical intent rather than exact simulation rules.

- It consistently describes **one operator per loop**, continuous circulation of inventory totes, a closed loop with buffers, dedicated inventory infeed and order outfeed zones, and middleware coordination of tote queues and robot arrival/dispatch.
- It says order-tote bots receive completed order totes at the outfeed and dispatch on order completion. That wording is too coarse for the Walmart order-container lifecycle in the later orchestration design and Daniel's current direction; it should not override the modeled order HTM presenting the order container for picks and then traveling to extraction.
- The page supplies no FIFO guarantee, no six-order assignment target, no order waiting-block capacity, and no authoritative pick duration. It therefore cannot answer the `W` versus `W + dispatch cover` question.

### Walmart PoT — Easy loop Station Orchestration Design

Source: [Confluence page](https://greyorange-work.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GRYMTTR/pages/72723168/Walmart+PoT+-+Easy+loop+Station+Orchestration+Design), owned by Kushal Awadh Saini, updated 2026-02-20. It is explicitly **DRAFT**, contains unfinished interfaces, placeholder acceptance mappings, and many open questions. Its detailed queue rules are valuable hypotheses, not settled requirements.

- It distinguishes the order side as **Waiting Point/racetrack → Active Bin (also called induct point) → extraction**. This is distinct from the inventory conveyor's APP. A single glossary should preserve: APP = inventory presentation point; Active Bin/induct point = order-container presentation; Waiting Point/Waiting Block = resequencable order staging.
- The orchestrator consumes the inventory-tote drop sequence and writes `order_container_requirement` entries. All containers associated with one inventory tote receive the same sequence number; the sequence increments for the next tote. Transport requests for order containers are created in sequence. This provides an ordering key tied to non-resequencable inventory arrival, while order containers can be repositioned to meet it.
- After a partial pick, the draft proposes: hold if the needed tote is at the front/already at PPS; move the order container back to the waiting block if at least two containers are ahead; rotate within the order queue if one container is ahead; hold if none are ahead. Completion sends Active Bin to extraction, frees Active Bin and Waiting Block, and promotes the next containers. This supports Daniel's distinction—inventory tote order is an input; order HTMs are the resequencing actuator—but the exact thresholds and examples need scenario tests because the draft has inconsistencies (one example table repeats `c2`, and its numbered flow skips Step 3).
- Its “Happy Flow” allows four outcomes after picking: return to second active-bin queue position, return to the PPS racetrack, extraction, or hold. It also describes terminal orders (cancelled, abandoned, unfulfillable, complete/short-pick) as cancelling pending transport and going to extraction. Daniel's current request to bypass the order buffer and send finished order totes directly to extraction is a valid experiment simplification; the wiki's richer lifecycle should remain disabled rather than partially mixed into that experiment.
- Capacity and admission are unresolved. The draft relies on the physical waiting point/racetrack and queue order, but defines no invariant for “orders assigned to station,” no proof that active assignments equal six continuously, and no CONWIP target above physical waiting capacity. Model these as separate quantities: assigned order work, dispatched order HTMs, admitted HTMs in the station footprint, occupied waiting cells, and active-bin occupancy. Then the proposed time series can reveal dispatch cover without treating every assigned order as a robot already admitted.
- The draft acknowledges race conditions between orchestration startup and inventory-sequence events, missing/finalizing topic contracts, task cancellation gaps, and missing sector-resolution behavior. Avoid encoding its database/event names as simulation domain concepts.

## Planning implications for station orchestration

- Model the operator as one exclusive station resource. A pick starts only when the correct inventory tote is usable at an inventory APP and the correct order container is usable at its Active Bin; the pick task holds the operator and both presentations for that task. Daniel's current note calls this 10 s, while the current implementation review reports an explicit 9 s operator task, so make the duration one named input and resolve that calibration deliberately. Conveyor tilt/changeover, HTM travel, extraction delay, and order resequencing remain separate activities and resources; in particular, do not reuse the pick duration as the extraction delay merely because both have recently been 10-second-scale settings.
- Preserve inventory conveyor arrival order as the exogenous ordered sequence. FIFO intent may explicitly skip or requeue an unavailable head tote; that is conveyor lifecycle control, not arbitrary inventory resequencing. Select and move order HTMs against the resulting sequence.
- Treat six waiting cells as physical admission capacity, not automatically as the assignment horizon. Maintain the user-requested invariant that at least six orders are assigned whenever six or more unassigned orders remain, and separately evaluate a configurable assignment/dispatch cover above six. Admission to the six-cell block stays capped at six. Metrics should expose assigned, dispatched/in transit, admitted, waiting-cell occupied, and active-bin counts over time.
- For the temporary isolation run, completed order containers go directly from the station presentation to extraction. Keep the order-buffer/release machinery out of that causal path so buffer capacity and delayed extraction cannot masquerade as station-internal backpressure.
